# PartnerCompany Model

Represents a partner company that participates in a client's incentive programs. Partner companies contain partner admins and sellers.

## Default company admin

Eight `admin*` fields, all nullable, describing the person who owns the company's payout account at the
payment provider. They arrive in **two stages, from two different people**.

**Identity — five fields, supplied by the client admin on create.** `adminFirstName`, `adminLastName`,
`adminEmail`, `adminMobileNumber`, `adminCountryIso2`, **as a group: all five or none**. A partial group is
rejected with `422 INVALID_ADMIN_DETAILS`. Supplying the full group creates the admin's **login** — a
PARTNER_ADMIN user with an onboarding token, through the same flow as any other user. It does *not* create
the payout account.

**Address — three fields, supplied by the admin themselves.** `adminCity`, `adminRegion`,
`adminPostalCode`, through `PUT /api/v1/company-admin/profile` after they sign in. That call is what
provisions the payout account. The address is not accepted on create: the provider binds the account to
`adminEmail` permanently and will not reuse the address, so the person who owns it is the one who types it.
A client admin guessing at someone else's address is the mistake this split exists to prevent.

**Only that one admin may complete or view the setup.** Other admins of the same company are refused with
403, on the read as well as the write. This cannot be a permission: every company admin holds the same
shared PARTNER_ADMIN role, so the server matches the caller against `adminEmail` instead. They keep full
distribution rights — they simply cannot set up or change the account that funds it. There is one
beneficiary per company, and its address fields live on this shared row, so a second admin completing the
form would leave an account whose address belongs to one person and whose email belongs to another.

They are columns rather than `metadata` entries deliberately: they are a vendor integration's input, and a
wrong `adminCountryIso2` fails a payout.

## Payout account

A partner company may have one payout-provider account, exposed on detail responses as `xtrmAccount`
(`status`, `accountNumber`, `identityLevel`, `lastError`). It **never** carries credentials.

`status` is the per-company enablement switch — there is no separate flag, so the two cannot disagree:

- `PENDING` — not yet fully provisioned. Holds whatever has been learned so far, including why the last
  attempt failed. Cannot pay.
- `CONNECTED` — may pay its sellers from its own wallet.
- `DISABLED` — switched off deliberately.

Deleting a partner company deletes this record with it. Nothing is deleted at the provider.

## Relationships

- Belongs to one Client (tenant)
- Has many Users (one-to-many via `users.partner_company_id`)
- Has many PartnerCompanyLocations (one-to-many — location assignments)

## Constraints

- Unique constraint: `(client_id, name)` — no two partner companies with the same name within a client
- One payout-provider account per partner company (`uq_xtrm_account_per_company`). Two rows would mean two
  possible funding sources for the same company's payouts, with nothing to say which is authoritative.

## API Response Fields

When returned in API responses, include all fields plus:

- `clientName` as a string (convenience field)

## Notes

- This entity is tenant-scoped — all queries filter by `client_id`.
- Users with `action.partner_company.create/edit/delete` permissions can manage partner companies.
